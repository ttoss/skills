# Chase

`/flow <project id> --chase` runs the [Chase](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ttoss/ttoss/main/docs/website/docs/engineering/workflow/routines.md#standard-routines). What the digest holds, its sections, their order and their size, is that page's; this one is how each section is read. The run works on no task and writes nothing to Flow but its run, recorded in section 0 with `agent: flow chase`; it needs `get-next`, `get-task`, `get-project`, `get-project-metrics`, `list-tasks`, `list-agent-runs`, `upsert-agent-run`, `validate-project`, the code host's read of changes and the delivery's tool.

**Read the Chase's row in the `Routines` parameter first**, in the project's parameters file that its entry section names, and load the delivery's tool before step 1: the row says where the digest goes, and a run that never read it ends with the digest in a final message nobody reads.

**Read each section by its own query, sorted so that what qualifies comes first, and stop at the first row that does not**: never the whole graph. A page whose last row still qualifies is read on, by the next page where the tool pages, else the section says it may be incomplete.

1. **Critical bugs**: `list-tasks` with `tag: [bug]`, `priority: [very_high, high]` and the open statuses, and `list-tasks` with `priority: [very_high]`. For each, what holds it: a wait (its reason, and who it is `on`), a task that `blocks` it, a `running` run holding it (one `list-agent-runs` with `project`, `status: [running]` and `limit` 100 for every bug, matched by `works_on`), or nothing ("ready, nobody took it"). A bug held only by a `flow tick` or `flow child` run started within `Stale after` is the agents' and makes no line of its own: those are one line, `N with agents, on time`; every other bug is a line.
2. **Changes waiting to be integrated by a person**: on the code host, each repo's open, non-draft changes whose checks and the review the `Review` parameter names passed; keep a change whose task (its URL in the description) is `waiting` on a person.
3. **No owner, overdue checks and overdue waits**: the one section no sorted read serves, since `sort: until` orders only `scheduled` dates and a row does not carry whom its wait is `on`. Read every external wait: `list-tasks` with `project`, `status: [waiting]`, `state: [blocked:external]` and `limit` 100, following `next_cursor` to the end. From each row's `readiness.blocked_by` wait, keep a task whose `until` has passed (overdue by that many whole days), or whose wait has no `until` and whose `review_due_at` has passed (no chase date, untouched for a week); a `decision` row is step 4's. `get-task` only each task kept, for whom its wait is `on` and its `implements` changes, and sort it:
   - **No owner**: its wait is `on` nobody;
   - **Overdue checks**: its `implements` change is integrated (the code host says so), so what is left is its check; also every task `get-next` with `slice: date_came` still lists, since the tick's check children would have taken it;
   - **Overdue waits**: the rest.
4. **Old decisions**: `get-next` with `kind: [decision]`, `status: [waiting]`, `assignee: ["any"]` and `review_due: true`, the one with the most `unblocks` first, then the oldest.
5. **Stale sessions**: `list-agent-runs` with `project`, `status: [running]`, `until` set to now minus the `Runtime`'s `Stale after` (default 4 hours) and `limit` 100; keep a run whose `agent` is not `flow tick`, `flow child` or `flow chase` (the tick closes stale children itself); each open task in its `works_on` is a line under the person who recorded it (`by`).
6. **Findings and due reviews**: what [review](https://flow.ttoss.dev/docs/method/review) asks a person to look at that no earlier section reaches, read here and never acted on (GTF-R1 to GTF-R3 are the person's run's).
   - **Findings**: one `validate-project` call. Drop `wait_without_on`, which step 3's No owner already reads; each other finding is a line on the task it names (`task`, `to`, `record`, or the first of `records`), its action the one `validate-project` gives for its `check`. Who acts is that task's `assignee`, by `get-task` on each task kept, else the project's `admin` members.
   - **Due reviews**: `get-next` with `project`, `slice: review`, `assignee: ["any"]` and `limit` the section's size plus the tasks the earlier sections took, the longest overdue first; its `total` is the "and N more". A task an earlier section took is left out. Who acts is its `assignee`, a team's members when it is a team, else the project's `admin` members; what they do is confirm what still holds, saying what they checked, or change it (GTF-R2).
7. **Health**: the changes the code host integrated since the newest earlier `flow chase` run started, `get-next`'s ready `total`, how many ready tasks a stale session holds, and how many of the waits kept in step 3 name nobody. Then the **planner's use**: of the `flow tick` runs since the newest earlier `flow chase` run, read with `list-agent-runs` (`agent: flow tick`, `since`, `limit: 100`), how many have a `summary` opening, after its `flow tick: ` label, with neither `plan:` nor `fallback:`, and how many have none, named by `url`: each skipped the planner or did not report. The `summary` is the tick's own word, a signal and not proof; 100 rows returned means the count may be short, and the line says so. Then the **flow's health**, read as Flow's [Reading a project's health](https://flow.ttoss.dev/docs/practice/health) reads it and as the console's health view draws it. Four `get-project-metrics` calls: this week (`until` now, `since` 7 days before) and the week before (`until` and `since` 7 days earlier), each once plain and once with `tag: [bug]`. Each row is a state as Flow names it, its `status`, `state` and `on`, labelled with the stage it reads as: **a person** when `on` is true; **the gates** for `waiting` / `blocked:external` without `on`; **ready** for `active` / `ready`; **a release** for `blocked`; **the check** for `calendar`; **incubated** for `incubated`, which the Audit reads, not the Chase. A row is **growing** in a week when more `entered` than `left`, **shrinking** when fewer, **steady** when as many.
   - **Queues** ([Q11](https://ttoss.dev/docs/product/product-development/principles/managing-queues#q11-the-cumulative-flow-principle-use-cfds-to-monitor-queues), [W21](https://ttoss.dev/docs/product/product-development/principles/applying-wip-constraints#w21-the-principle-of-the-critical-queue-constrain-wip-in-the-section-of-the-system-where-the-queue-is-most-expensive)): a line per row that had an open task, an entry or an exit in either week, the rows with `on` true first: its `open`, this week's `entered` and `left` and the week before's, its direction, the p90 of `open_days` and the p50 of `left_days`, such as `a person (waiting · blocked:external · on): 9 open · 6 in, 4 out (5 in, 5 out the week before) · growing · p90 11d · p50 left 2d`.
   - **The person queue's alert**: a row with `on` true growing in both weeks is the decision bottleneck widening, which adding agents or tasks will not help. The digest says so in its opening, addressed to the project's `admin` members, before the lines per person.
   - **Delivery**: `completed`'s count and its p50 and p90 days, this week and the week before.
   - **Rework** ([FF3](https://ttoss.dev/docs/product/product-development/principles/using-fast-feedback#ff3-the-principle-of-leading-indicators-select-control-variables-that-predict-future-system-behavior)): each `moves` row to `active` from the gates or from the check, its `count` this week and the week before. Back from the check is read first: the change passed every gate and its effect was still not there.
   - **Failure demand**: the `tag: [bug]` calls' rows, as the queue lines, beside the plain calls' `completed` count.

   Every number is one Flow answers, written as answered: never add two rows together, divide one number by another, or compute one from task rows; a `null` spread is "no data", never 0. No number is a target, so a line carries a direction and no verdict, goal or threshold. A deployment without the tool, or an error, leaves these lines out and says so in one line.
8. **Write the digest** in the project's language, in the shape the routines page gives: a title line with the date, the person queue's alert when step 7 raised one, then the lines per person, the sections and the health lines. Each line is `[title](url) — <who acts> — <what they do, from the wait's reason or the change>`; "holding the most tasks" is `unblocks`, or the tasks its `get-task` links `blocks`. Who acts is the person the wait is `on`, the run's `by`, the person the `Merge rule` names, or, for No owner, the project's `admin` members (`get-project`), who name the owner; name each by their email in Flow (`get-project`), with their first name in parentheses: `pedro@example.com (Pedro)`. Never an `@handle`: a delivery sent as text does not turn it into a mention.
9. Deliver it where the Chase's row in `Routines` says, such as a channel; only a row that names no destination makes it the run's final message, which the runtime delivers to the routine's owner. A delivery that fails or is refused closes the run `failed`, with the reason in its final message beside the digest.
10. The `Runtime`'s acts on every run, then close the run. The final message is the digest, or one line naming where it went and how many lines it holds.

Never move a task, change a wait or its `until`, or send anything but the one digest.
